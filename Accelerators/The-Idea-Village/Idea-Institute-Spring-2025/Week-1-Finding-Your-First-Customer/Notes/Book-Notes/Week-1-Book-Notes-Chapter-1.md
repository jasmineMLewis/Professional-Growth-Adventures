# 🏢 1.1 Mini Case: The Rise of Bagel Boy
The Rise of Bagel Boy is about a college student who recognize a need in a lack of bagel market in New Orleans, by initially delivering free one day old bagel to his college friends for free. He would contact them on Facebook and bike to deliver them. He expanded by posting his one day old bagels on social media. Eventually, one of his friends referred to him as "Bagel Boy." He would travel to deliver them and some customers would provide him a tip. He conducted interview by asking his current customers would they pay for his service. He learned that it was his convenient delivery that meet a need in the market. Followed by local coffee shop owners begin to wholesale order and he wanted to ensure these customers would pay. Eventually, he expanded to a small team and formalized his business, creating a rudimentary website, and process to for customers to order and delivery service.

# 💬 Orientation: Generating Ideas
When inspiration and customer traction with customer do not grow, two of the following things occur for the entrepreneur:
1. Lack of traction becomes a former of frustration and dissuade individuals from pursuing entrepreneurship
2. After losing confidence, the founder creates a duplicate venture of competitors in a crowded market.

With either situation, the founder loses motivation, interest, and resources. Chapter 1 focuses on increasing the likelihood that an entrepreneur's idea is feasible and actionable through idea generation processes. This process is iterative step that one must complete without interacting with other team members to flush out the idea. It is imperative to connect with other about your idea who is not in your network and who is a potential customer. Although you may have an idea, the idea generation process enables you to sharpen and strengthen your idea to be ready for an evolving market and customer motivation changes.

# 🛠️ 1.3 Step 1: Identifying Gaps
As founders, we create solutions based on our problems or experiences from our live, also known as "pain points". Paul Graham, founder of Y Combinator, an incubator that seeded Airbnb and Dropbox, suggests that “the way to get startup ideas is not to try to think of startup ideas. It’s to look for problems, preferably problems you have yourself.”

## Tier One Concerns
“Tier 1” Concerns —those that are top-of-mind for potential customers and that they want resolved immediately.

## Brainstorming
Teams have brainstorming sessions to generate a multiplicity of ideas. It allows for "collision of hunches" generated together by individuals invested trying to explore opportunities. ***Brainstorming Sessions*** ensure teams capture as many good ideas as possible.

***Brainstorming*** is a structured ideation process that you have probably participated in at some point in your life.

### Brainstorming Structured Guidelines
1. Nominate a facilitator responsible for documented ideas and maintain contributes in an orderly manner.
2. Do not criticize ideas, even if it appears silly. It can be a launch point.
3. Verbalize all ideas without fear of criticism or self-censorship due to doubting perceived quality or worthiness.
4. Members are encourage to combine ideas and build upon each other's ideas to develop further.
5. Members should agree as a group all ideas created are commonly own by the group. 

By following these guidelines and having fun, ideas created as a group will be more appealing than a single person can create. Brainstorming Sessions when done well can make group members feel involved and appreciated.

## Job to be Done
A way to frame search for “Tier 1” Concerns is to state a gap in terms of  **Job To Be Done**. By understanding the **Job To Be Done** for which customers want to "hire" a product or service, founders can more accurately develop solutions tailored to what customers are already trying to do.

# 📜 Step 2: Matching Jobs to Elegant Solutions
The jobs to be done you just identified are potential **opportunities.**  Opportunities come into being when there is legitimate interest on the part of a group of customers to fill a gap or tackle a job to be done. In this step of the idea generation process, you are going to create solutions that fit the jobs to be done. There are two key tensions to need to consider and balance before solutions are created:
1. Tension 1: Focused, yet Scalable
    - Creating a "Beachhead" Persona is essential. Microsoft founders focused on core early adopters of technology who could afford expensive software and machines. They discovered these customers did not want basic or generic features. It did not satisfy a large, heterogeneous group, with different preferences, but a core market. 
2. Tension 2: Satisfying, Not Overwhelming
    - Your solution must address the **Job To Be Done** in a substantive way. Focus on solving their problem. Do not introduce more problems, nor overwhelm with features.
 

# 📈 1.5 Route Monitoring: Confirming Your Idea's Potential
After thinking of solutions, use the previous steps to evaluate your idea's potential.
1. Urgency
    - If your solution isn't urgent to solving the problem and customers are not willing to pay now, it is best to go back and evaluate your solution.
2. Scalability
    - If your solution only solves a small market, you may need to reconsider whether or not you want a small business such as a "mom and pop" shop or a small website design shop.
3. Profitability
    - Determine whether or not the profit margins for your business is low or high.
4. Actionability 
    - If your idea requires upfront capital for a novel technology that will require research and development, and may or may not be possible, you may want to reconsider. Or if it is a website that you can hire developers to create in a short amount of time, you may want to choose that. If depends on understanding your capabilities and a time horizon for success.
5. Originality
    - Research to see if you have a similar competitor tackling your current problem with a solution. Act as if you are the customer attempting to resolve your problem. These are two websites you can use to research if other startups are tackling a similar customer problem:
        - ![Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com)
        - ![AngelList](https://angel.co)
6. Marketability
    - Research to learn if the market you are entering is a mature or growing. Entrepreneurs have success in markets where "the pie" is growing quickly.
 
# 📋 1.6 Step 3: Crafting a Positioning Statement
A _**Positioning statement**_ is a concise description of your beachhead market and should deliver a compelling picture of how you want customers in that market to perceive your solution. Your _**Positioning statement**_ may sound like a promotional marketing material, it can be used as an internal tool.

> Every product and marketing decision you make should align with and support your positioning statement. A _**Good Positioning statement**_  helps you maintain focus on your brand and its value proposition while you work on a sensible go-to-market strategy.

## Positioning Statement Template
1. For (clear description target customer segment):
2. who (have the following job to be done):
3. our (product/service name):
4. is a (type of solution or product category):
5. that (statement of value, benefit, breakthrough capability):
6. unlike (reference competition or current, alternative solution):
7. our product/solution (does what: the key point of competitive differentiation):

## Sample Positioning Statements
### Online Shopfront Marketers
For *online shopfront marketers* (target customer segment) who struggle to find return on investment in social media (problem/job to be done), Milia (name) is a web-based analytics tool that compiles and translates engagement metrics into actionable, prioritized task lists (type of solution). Unlike Google Analytics (competition), our software captures data across several search engines and social media platforms, providing customers with a reliable, comprehensive view of their business that they can act on immediately (key point of differentiation).

### Recent College Graduates
For *recent college graduates* who have trouble making new friends after leaving campus, Friendline is a mobile app that connects individuals with shared tastes and social preferences to each other without awkward introductions or false starts. Unlike sports leagues and other activity-based, friend-finding solutions, our focus is on matching customers with friends who share similar interests beyond sports and drinking.
