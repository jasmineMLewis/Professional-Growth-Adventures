# 🏢 Mini Case: Luna Finds Its Focus
The Lunas are passionate about nature and offered an about of services to different customer segments initially. They enjoyed the work but discovered that after a few years, their revenue has not increased and they were always busy with a project, workshop, or other service they offered. They did not have time for personal activities that sparked their creative passion for nature. Therefore, they decided to focus on a single customer segment: high end design clients that had a universal appeal. To acquire more customers, they offered invitation-only workshops that they used as a form to reach developers and designers as opposed as a revenue source. By finding clarity of their customer segment, it was easier to focus.

# 2.2 🔍 Orientation: Customer Discovery
_**A Job to be Done**_ is identified and suggested, in broad strokes, and who might need that job done. A clear and pressing job to be done is a great starting point for a discussion about a potential business, but everyone will not purchase your solution. Some founders discover their product's solution is not pressing enough for their market to purchase after they invested time and resources.

## Systematic Approach to Understand Your Customer's Needs
A way to prevent this is to be empathetic to your customer needs and stepping into their shoes.
Things to consider:
- What they really want rather than what they say they want?
- Characteristics (e.g., demographics, availability of funds)
- Behaviors (e.g., when and how they buy products and services)

By learning to deeply understand your customer, you can begin to take steps to impress customers with a solution instead of being surprised by how quickly they disregard your solution.

## Chapter 2 Objective
The objective of Chapter 2 is to help you not just avoid building a solution to fit a customer that is mildly interested in your solution but to identify a customer segment that is wholly interested in working with you to accomplish a job to be done.

# 2.3 🧑‍💼 Step 1: Describing a Useful Customer Persona
A _**Persona**_ is humanized description of a customer that includes details that might not be obviously pertinent to the business but that clue one in to how they live and make buying decisions.

## Usage of Personae
Personae are standard tools used to properly, they provide a mental model of a customer that managers and employees can refer to as they make decisions about how to build and sell their products and services. A well-articulated persona can help keep founders focused on what matters to a customer who really needs a job to be done tackled and who is willing to pay well for it. Demographics, although useful information, does not provides enough details to craft a discussion or product test.

## Crafting a Useful Persona Profile
### Step 1
- Go back to the **Positioning Statement**  you created in Chapter 1.
- Try to visualize the person that is most interested in the **"Job to Be Done."** Whom is this urgent, high priority problem?
- Give the customer a **Role** by creating a backstory
    - This enables you to have a viable reason for why this persona might have an interest in your solution

### Step 2
- Create a brief **Role Description** by using your customer created backstory

### Step 3
- Expand the narrative by crafting a broad description of the persona’s frustration or situation (a backstory)

### Step 4
- Add likely **Demographic** variables that you think might constrain your target market (e.g., age, gender, income, geographic location)

### Step 5
- Capture any **Psychographic** differences that might give you a more nuanced view of the customer
    - **Psychographics**  measure customers' attitudes and interests rather than objective demographic criteria

<br/>
After drafting a persona, you will probably have a strong sense of all the things you do not know or suspect you know, but have not observed. A conscientious founder should be constantly updating and tweaking a handful of personae—usually three or four for a new venture—as the venture gets off the ground.

## Different Personas
### First Persona
Most businesses cannot grow by relying too heavily on a customer type for very long. You want to have a backup in case you are unable to find versions of your first persona in the real world when you start conducting interviews.

### Second Persona
Make a second persona distinct from the first persona for it to be helpful in guiding founding team discussions or formulating marketing campaigns.

## Multisided Market
_**Multisided Market**_ , sometimes called Platforms, serve two or more end users at the same time. If you are creating a **Multisided Market**, it is necessary to create **Personas** for each side. Creating customer profiles give team your team focus. 

# 🧭 2.4 Route Monitoring: Persona Reality Check
Creating **Personas** are fun, but it is important to ensure it is an informed, reasonable guess. To ensure your early-stage personae are viable, they must exhibit these five characteristics:
1. Realistic
    - You can coherently describe your persona in a way that allows someone unfamiliar with your product or service to believe that person exists in the real world. The characteristics such as demographics and descriptors must be believable.
2. Sizable
    - A **Persona** cannot have a small business market and it must be cost-effective to target those customer segments. A **Persona**  must be representative of a customer segment large enough to support, perhaps in combination with one or two other personae, a business in its first two years of existence.
3. Accessible
    - Your company should be able to reach its customer segments via communication and distribution channels. If you are entering the market, consider whether or not you have connections necessary to gain access to your ideal customer base.
4. Stable
    - For a marketing campaign to be successful, a customer type should be stable enough for a long enough period to be marketed to strategically.
5. Distinguishable
    - Your personae may have similar problems that are tackled by your solution. They must, however, also have clear distinguishing characteristics that influence their behavior, such as their ability or willingness to purchase. If they are largely similar, it is not worthwhile splitting the focus of your founding team across multiple customer types. Instead, start with a single persona and let other profiles emerge from customer interviews and early sales.
<br/>
*Remember, a persona is just a hypothesis that will be revised several times before you find a business model that takes off.*

# 2.5 🎯 Destination: Reflection and Next Steps
Personae are useful only if they are kept alive by a founding team. They must become part of day-to-day conversations. They should be easy to deploy as examples to illustrate why adopting a product feature or promotion makes sense.

## Other Uses for Personae
### Location-based variables
As your persona profile gains complexity, consider gaining a better understanding, of other variables, such as location-based variables. Knowing your customer location can help you decide, for instance, whether radio advertising or highway billboard advertising is at all appropriate.
1. Do your customers live in urban, suburban, or rural areas?
2. Do they commute between home and work?

### Other Products or Services
It is important to know what other products or services they buy regularly or are particularly excited about purchasing. The part of this person’s life you are trying to understand most deeply is how they find out about products or services and how they decide on a purchase. It can be helpful to compile a short list of other brands this persona interacts with or cares about.
1. What other products or services they buy regularly or are particularly excited about purchasing?
2. What other brands this persona interacts with or cares about?
3. How does your brand compare to the brands the persona is already satisfied with?
