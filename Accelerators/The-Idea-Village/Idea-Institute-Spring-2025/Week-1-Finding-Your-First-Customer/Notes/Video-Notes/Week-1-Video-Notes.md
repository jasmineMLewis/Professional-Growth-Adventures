# 💼 Customer Segment
By picking one customer segment and really understanding that customer, you increase your chances of being able to wow that customer. It helps you stand out in a crowded field of potential competitors.

# 📈 Scaling
## Identify a Problem & a Solution
Before a company can scale and grow, a founder my identify a customer problem and a solution. More importantly, a problem they are willing to pay for to have solve.

## Product Market Fit
Before the startup can begin to scale or grow, founding teams must devise solution (e.g., a product or service) that a specific segment of paying customers cannot live without!

You need to find a product that specific customers are willing to pay to have the problem solved for them.

Reaching **Product Market Fit** can be extremely tricky, even for experienced entrepreneurs.

# 📉 Common Pitfalls
Some mistake that leads startups include:
### Being a "clone" company
Entering a crowded market with limited resources. This often comes from following a fad that has already seen its best days (e.g, cupcake shops).
- More than likely entering a market that is heavily saturated.
- The first business has a first mover Advantage. The customers already know them and they know what they can do. You are going to be left with the second cupcake shop, and investors do not like to see.

### Thinking you can sell to everyone
Believing that even though you have limited time and resources, you can sell to everyone (e.g., "everyone will be interested in this solution")
- By spreading out all your marketing, resources, and efforts to all these customer segments you are not going to create something that wow that customer and keep them coming back.

# 🧑‍💼 Know Your Customer
Customers seldom fit into a pre-determined mold and are often harder to profile than one expects when launching a startup.

A rich and nuanced understanding of your customer persona and how they can be reached and compelled into adopting a new solution is fundamental.

- Think carefully about who that customer is and try to develop a rich profile, add detail and nuance to a profile for that customer. 
- You should be only making decisions from your perspective.
- A key to this process is to be able to step into your customer's shoes and imagine the world from their perspective.
- It helps to have a detail profile so you can understand their problem deeply and who they are.

## Building Customer Personas
When painting a rich picture of a target customer, entrepreneurs often limit themselves to describing 
**Demographics** (age, gender, income) + **Psychographics** (personality, values, opinions, attitudes, interests, and lifestyles). 

These can be useful, but do not stop there. Add nuance if it is (1) useful to understanding how and why a customer make decisions and (2) useful to you as you make resource allocation decisions.

## Distinction between the End User and Economic Buyer
The person using the solution might not be the person paying for it:

### End User
The person who ultimately uses the product or service offered by the entrepreneur

### Economic Buyer
Person who pays for the product

**Example**
- Managers may buy products that are going to be used by frontline employees
- Parents may buy toys for their kids and never experience the full value of the product themselves (e.g, Lego bricks)

## Platforms
Some startups must serve more than one customer group

Multi-sided markets connected two or more customer groups to each other. The value is typically in facilitating the connections and transactions that occur between segments.
- If you are building a platform of multiple **Personas** you need to understand both sides of the market well as well as what value you add as a platform to their interaction.

**Example**
For instance: Companies connect buyers and sellers (e.g., Ebay), couples (e.g, Match.com), or pet owners and pet sitters (e.g, Rover) by structuring transactions in safe, repeatable ways.

# 💰 Beachhead Persona
Ideally, the first persona you will target with your limited marketing budget - your beachhead persona - will share the following characteristics:
1. They want to **buy very similar solutions**. In effect, there will be limited variance needed in production or service delivery to "wow" these customers.
2. They will **buy similar channels and follow similar sales cycles**. It would be tricky to manage a direct sales force targeting large contracts and a social media team targeting individuals making micro-transactions.
3. There is **"word of mouth" between customers** - they know who to share news of your solution with and the news spreads quickly.

## Tier One Problems
- **Tier One Problems** are the kinds of problems that people are thinking about regularly and that they would pay good money to solve.
- A Tier One needs to solve the problem that you are looking to solve.

## Guidelines
If you follow these guidelines and if you think about what **Beachhead Persona** are ideal across multiple dimensions, it should make it a lot easier for your to make a decision about who to go to first.

A. Economic Buyer + End User -> University Students: Graduate and Undergrad Students

# 🎯 Today's Takeaways
## 01
Many startups fail because they don't spend enough time identifying and enriching their knowledge of a specific customer segment that urgently needs their solution. Many fail because they do not step into their customer shoes. The better developed your **Customer Persona** is beyond demographics and psychographics to really understanding behavior, the better off you will be.

## 02
A rich, nuanced **Beachhead Customer Persona** will allow you to make better resource allocation decisions and create a solution that "wows". A **Good Customer Persona** help you make decisions both about where to put your limited marketing money and resource allocation decisions as well as how to wow that customer so that customer talks about you with other people.

## 03
Markets are often structured in unique ways. Think about identifying end users, economic buyers, and decision makers - they may not be the same person! So, consider your end user and who will be using your product, who is going to be making the purchasing decisions about that product, and who is the actual decision maker about whether that product or service will be used or not.
