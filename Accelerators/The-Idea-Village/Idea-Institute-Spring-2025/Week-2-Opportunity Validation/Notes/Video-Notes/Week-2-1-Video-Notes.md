# 📉 Common Pitfalls
---

### Settling for Hypotheses
- Many founders stop searching and settle on a handful of personae that they believe will be into their solution. Hypothesizing a persona is the beginning and not the end of your journey.
- <u>A persona is only a hypothesis that needs to be tested</u> before it can become the basis for an entire business.
- That is why you need customer discovery:
    - Does the problem or job-to-be-done you identified resonate with people who fit your persona profile?
    - Do people who fit your persona profile exist in the real world?

<br/>

**Customer Discovery** 
Take a step back and imagine your customer going through their day-to-day life, then turn those musing into questions. Real questions you can ask your customer. This is the process of **Customer Discovery.**

<br/>
You imagined your customer, but have not whether or not they exiperence the problem or job to be done. Following this, is it urgent enough or significant enough to make it worth it for you to start a business.

<br/>
**Customer Discovery** is an important of the process and key to the Lean Startup Method.

# 🚀 Lean Startup Method
The Lean Startup Method, developed by individuals such as Eric Reese and Steve Blank, is that startups typically proposed their business to investors. It allows the founders to complete their due diligence with customers by making small bets and not big ones by making small bets along the way, such as the customer matches your hypothesized persona. 
- By working in mini steps, you are de-risking your startup. 
- Starting a business is not about taking risks. It about making small bets that de-risk the process



# 🔍 Customer Discovery Data
---

Customer Discovery have two categories:
- Self Report Methods
- Non-Self Report Methods

## Self-Report Methods
1. **One-on-One Interviews**
2. Focus Groups
3. Surveys
4. Online Polls

You are asking customers about their day-to-day life, the problems as they experience them, and trying to figure out if your solution is a good match for that customer.

### One-on-One Interviews
One-on-One Interviews are the most useful in the **Customer Discovery** process. It is an efficient way to determine:
- Who your customer is
- If they match your persona
- If there is an opportunity worth pursuing there

**One-on-one Interviews** are the core of numerous methods individuals use to start their businesses. The reason is because you can engage in a conversation with someone who matches the customer's profile and discover things you would not be able to discover using a survey method, as an example.

<br/>

*Key* to a good **One-on-one Interview** is to unearth the unexpected. The reason for **Customer Discovery** is not only to validate your own position or understanding of the customer, but to stay open to unexpected information that you might discover through conversation.

<br/>

**One-on-one Interviews** are about unexpected information.

## Non-Self Report Methods
1. Observation
2. Experiments (e.g., A/B testing)
3. Archival (e.g., Collecting data on Online Presence)


## Triangulation Methods of Customer Discovery Data
Great customer data comes not only though careful collection of interview data, but from the triangulation of interview data with other data sources and methods.

<br />

By comparing and contrasting the weaknesses of methods and using the strengths to complement each other, you gather useful and amazing information.

### Steps for can use to Maximize Methods
- If you start with one-on-one interviews and slowly develop the questions you need to create a survey.
- Through the survey you might increase the number of people you speak with
- Then you may go to your associated location for your business idea to see if the associated claims made by the people made in the interviews and surveys pan out int the real world when they're not self-reporting

### Why Triangulation Is Essential
A founder should partake in the **Triangulation of Customer Discovery** and not have a marketing firm nor only co-founders to conduct by themselves. The triangulation of different methods will help you develop:
- A sense of where the need is,
- Where the pain points are,
- What the problem is,
- What the job to be done is that needs to be tackled.

Without you exerting unnecessary time making mistakes that can be expensive and that could lead to the failure of your startup.

# 📝 Interview Protocol
---

An **Interview Protocol** usually consists of a list of questions that you want to ask your potential customers. It ranges from 10 - 20 questions you bring to the interview so you can look down at and make you are tackling all the question that you have that will help validate the opportunity.

<br/>

Follow a few guidelines when designing your protocol:
- Use open ended questions that allow customers to elaborate with stories. Avoid having questions that are amendable to yes/no responses.
    - Ask questions that are generative to get the interviewee talking.
    - Try not to ask questions that are very not generic or get the person not to focus on what you want to talk about.
- Come up with a brief explanation of why you are conducting the interview and make sure to tell the interviewee how the information they provide will be used.
    - You also want to start the interview and want to explain why you are there and information you are gathering is going to be used for.
    - First job is to get the person comfortable in talking about their day-to-day life regarding your domain.
    - Let them know your team and you are working on a problem and would like for them to give you a realistic perspective.
    - This is better than stating you are starting a business and you would like to discuss your product.
    - *Remember* the customer is the STAR of the show. Treat them like the expert and they'll give you great answers.
- You might want include some potential follow-up questions that might be used to prompt customer under each open-ended question.
- Record the interview (with permission) so that your team can have a record of what was said.
    - This is important because you want to bring back nuggets of truth to get your team on board.
    - The more evidence you can provide evidence for not only investors, but your own startup's development, and get your team on board.
    - RESIST the temptation to ask them specifically about your solution! This is NOT a SALES CALL, but a DISCOVERY CALL.
    - Do not sell at all, even at the end of the interview. The customer has to believe you are integrating their feedback into your process.
    If you are showing them your solution, you're essentially undermining that feeling of co-creation that you might otherwise give your customer.


## Persona Discovery
1. What are your biggest/most professional responsibilities/goals?
2. Who handles [domain you are focusing on] at your home/office?
3. Tell me about your role at [company]?
4. [Specific questions related to your product/customer] - for example, do you have a carer aspiration? A favorite brand?

## Job-to-be-done Discovery
1. What's the hardest part of your day?
2. What are some unmet needs you have?
3. What tasks take up the most time in your day?
4. What could be done to improve your experience with the [process/role]?
5. What's the hardest part about being a [role]?

# 📋 Should I Use Surveys?
---

- Close-ended instruments like surveys and polls <u>should not be used until you know what questions to ask</u> (i.e., after enough interviews).
    - One-on-One interviews are not replaceable.
    - A survey, even with open ended questions, is not going to give you the kind of unexpected information that you can get by asking follow-up questions.
    - You might use a few one-on-one interviews to help you craft better survey questions that can supplement the responses and the findings that you get from interviews. 
- Some people use them as a recruiting tool for interviews to ensure that respondents match certain demographic or psychographic criteria (e.g by creating a landing page or social media presence that contains a short survey and email collection tool)
    - Some people use surveys as a way to filter people to make sure that they fit the Persona profile.
    - Several entrepreneurs post on social media with several questions that helps them filter people that they then pre-qualify for opportunity validation interviews.
    - It is a useful way to beginning building a list of potential customers to be used later.

# ⚠️ Important Advice
--- 

## 01
Focus on trying to extract stories and narratives from the customers by asking open-ended questions. Open-ended questions are questions that ask somebody about their day, what the toughest part of their day is, and not yes or no questions.

<br/>
Importantly, you should always start discovery process with interviews heavy on open-ended questions about customer experiences in a domain you are exploring. Doing do will give potential customers the leeway to answer questions with long form narratives that connect persona and job-to-be-done.
